*Strategic signals for Brainport Development. Every item with date and source.*

**The essence this week.** The European movement is visibly shifting from research to *industrialisation*: Germany is committing up to €640M through six competing consortia with hard milestones and a semiconductor pilot line for QPUs, and a global survey shows that 90% of quantum companies depend on foreign suppliers — with cryogenics, lasers and control electronics as bottlenecks. Exactly the layers where Brainport has something to offer. Locally there were two concrete Eindhoven signals (SMART Photonics–GlobalFoundries, CUbIQ–Coherent). And €119M of EuroHPC calls is open with a 17 November deadline, including pilot lines and test infrastructure.

## 1. Top signals

### 1. Germany commits up to €640M via six competing consortia; QUDORA gets €122M including a QPU pilot line

*23 Sep 2026 — [Quantum Spectator](https://quantumspectator.com/six-consortia-selected-to-compete-in-germanys-quantum-computing-competition-with-e640-million-in-planned-funding/) · [QUDORA](https://qudora.com/news/german-government-selects-qudora-led-consortium-for-e122-million-project-nfqc-1k-to-develop-a-1000-qubit-fault-tolerant-quantum-computer-and-build-a-qpu-pilot-line/) · [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/german-government-selects-qudora-led-consortium-for-e122-million-138-8-usd-nfqc-1k-trapped-ion-quantum-computer-project/)*

- **What:** The German ministry BMFTR selected six consortia for the Quantum Computing Competition, with up to €640M in total: LOGIQC (neutral atoms, planqc), NFQC-1k (trapped ions, QUDORA), ATHENA-X (superconducting, Peak Quantum), SPARQC (superconducting, with Eqcited), NEXUS (trapped ions, with neQxt) and SuperPilot (superconducting, IQM Germany). It is a staged competition: by 31 March 2027 each consortium must show a working system that meets the entry criteria; after 30 months a new selection round follows; by 2030 there must be at least two error-corrected quantum computers.
- **The NFQC-1k part:** €122M (~$138.8M) over five years to the consortium led by QUDORA Technologies (Braunschweig), with TU Braunschweig, Leibniz Universität Hannover, PTB, Forschungszentrum Jülich, **NXP Semiconductors Germany** and AQT Germany. Goal: at least 1,000 physical and 50 logical qubits with logical gate error rates below 0.01%, plus **a semiconductor pilot line for QPU production**. QUDORA uses chip-level microwave control instead of laser arrays, precisely because that fits standard semiconductor processes.
- **Why it matters:** This is the largest national quantum commitment in Europe right now, and the design is telling: not a broad distribution of grants but an elimination race with hard milestones, explicitly focused on *manufacturability* — pilot lines, semiconductor processes, industrial accessibility. With this, Germany is buying a position in the production of quantum hardware, not just in the physics. NXP's participation is the detail to remember.
- **Relevance:** Six funded consortia that must deliver working systems before March 2027 means acute demand for equipment, cryogenics, control electronics, packaging and test capacity — across the border, within driving distance. That is a sales market for Brabant suppliers rather than a threat. Worth testing: which of the six consortia have open supply needs, and is there an entry route via NXP Netherlands or existing German–Dutch cluster relationships?

### 2. SMART Photonics and GlobalFoundries open first open-access foundry for silicon + indium phosphide

*21 Sep 2026 — [PhotonDelta](https://www.photondelta.com/news/smart-photonics-globalfoundries-silicon-indium-phosphide-foundry/)*

- **What:** After more than a year of co-engineering, SMART Photonics (Eindhoven) and GlobalFoundries are launching an open-access foundry service that combines GF's silicon platform with SMART's InP lasers and optical amplifiers via flip-chip integration. Target markets: AI infrastructure, data centres, LiDAR, optical transceivers. General availability is planned for H2 2027.
- **Why it matters:** Strictly speaking this is a classical photonics announcement, not quantum. But it is the *manufacturing base* on which photonic quantum technology in Europe has to land: integrated light sources at wafer scale, via an accessible foundry model. An Eindhoven company linking up with one of the largest Western foundries is a structural strengthening of the region's position in the supply chain.
- **Relevance:** Directly Brainport. This increases the region's credibility as a location for photonic quantum and quantum communication companies looking for a production route. Also relevant as an argument in EU files on pilot lines (see section 2).

### 3. CUbIQ Technologies (Eindhoven) and Coherent demonstrate plug-in QKD for AI data centres

*21 Sep 2026 — [PhotonDelta](https://www.photondelta.com/news/cubiq-coherent-quantum-security-ai-infrastructure/)*

- **What:** At ECOC 2026 (Málaga, 21–23 Sep) CUbIQ demonstrated a Continuous-Variable QKD transceiver in QSFP-28 format, tested alongside Coherent's 200G FR4 transceivers on an NVIDIA DGX Spark system, without hardware modifications. Claim: about 100× lower energy, cost and footprint than rack-based QKD.
- **Why it matters:** Commercially, QKD mainly runs into cost and integration burden. A pluggable in a standard form factor changes that conversation: it becomes a purchasing decision instead of an infrastructure project. The link to AI data centre interconnects is moreover a market that is growing *now*, unlike many quantum use cases.
- **Relevance:** An Eindhoven company showing a market-ready quantum-safe solution together with a global photonics player. A concrete hook for the story "Brainport supplies quantum-enabling hardware that can be sold today". Also relevant for EuroQCI tracks and Dutch data centre/telecom operators.

### 4. Global survey: 90% of quantum companies depend on foreign suppliers

*23 Sep 2026 — [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/global-survey-finds-90-of-quantum-companies-rely-on-cross-border-supply-chains/)*

- **What:** A coalition of QED-C, Europe's QuIC, Quantum Industry Canada, UKQuantum, Q-STAR (Japan), the Korean quantum industry association and QURECA (India) surveyed 160 quantum companies in 15 countries. Findings: 90% rely on at least one foreign supplier for specialised hardware or software; 74% serve international customers; companies source from 36 countries, export to 49 and manufacture in 25. Largest dependencies: **cryogenics, lasers and control electronics**. Collectively the EU is the second-largest hub.
- **Why it matters:** This is the best-substantiated rebuttal of purely national sovereignty reflexes — and at the same time a map of where Europe is vulnerable. The bottleneck layers named are not qubits, but supply.
- **Relevance:** Cryogenics, lasers, control electronics, precision mechanics and test — that is largely Brabant and Twente territory. The report provides useful ammunition to position Brainport as a supplier in the European quantum chain rather than as a competitor of Delft or Munich. Recommended: request the full report via QuIC and compare the bottleneck list with the regional company base.

### 5. QuiX Quantum signs exclusive distribution deal for Japan

*24 Sep 2026 — [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/quix-quantum-partners-with-tohoku-electronic-industrial-for-exclusive-distribution-in-japan/)*

- **What:** Dutch photonic quantum company QuiX Quantum (Enschede) makes Tohoku Electronic Industrial (Sendai, founded 1968) its exclusive distributor in Japan for the Alquor 2.0 processor (8, 20 and 32 modes) and the Photonic Assembly Control Unit. TEI-C handles local sales, seminars and first-line support. Context: Japan is targeting ¥50 trillion in economic output and 10 million quantum users by 2030, with ¥100bn in additional national funding.
- **Why it matters:** A Dutch hardware supplier setting up a real distribution channel in a large, funded market — not an MoU, but a sales structure. That is one of the few commercially hard signals of this week.
- **Relevance:** Confirms that Dutch strength lies in photonic quantum hardware, the same technology base as the SMART Photonics and CUbIQ items above. An argument for a joint Dutch photonics-quantum proposition for export and EU calls.

### 6. Infleqtion achieves 30 entangled logical qubits on a neutral-atom processor

*24 Sep 2026 — [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/infleqtion-achieves-30-entangled-logical-qubits-on-sqale-neutral-atom-quantum-processor/) · [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/24/infleqtion-achieves-30-entangled-logical-qubits-on-its-sqale-quantum-computer/)*

- **What:** 80 physical neutral-atom qubits encode 30 logical qubits (overhead 8:3), with four non-Clifford logical CCZ gates and about 1 KiloQuOp of operations. AI-assisted circuit search halved the physical gate overhead per logical entanglement from eight to four two-qubit gates. Roadmap: 100 logical qubits in 2028, 1,000 in 2030.
- **Why it matters:** The overhead ratio is the most interesting number. 8:3 is remarkably favourable compared with the hundreds-to-one common for superconducting systems. Caution is warranted: this is a vendor announcement on its own benchmark, presented at a conference, without independent replication.
- **Relevance:** Indirect. If neutral atoms win the overhead race, demand for enabling components shifts towards optics, laser systems and vacuum technology — more favourable for Brabant manufacturing than an outcome in which cryogenic superconductivity dominates. It is one of the variables to follow in the coming years.

### 7. Pasqal publishes H1 2026: €4.9M revenue, loss up to €59.2M, €312.9M cash

*24 Sep 2026 — [Pasqal](https://www.pasqal.com/newsroom/pasqal-reports-first-half-2026-financial-results/) · [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/pasqal-reports-h1-2026-financial-results-e312-9m-post-spac-cash-balance-14-revenue-growth-and-1000-atom-scale/)*

- **What:** Revenue €4.9M (+14% y-o-y); QPU-related services revenue €3.9M (+34%). Operating loss rose from €19.8M to €59.2M, of which €27.3M share-based charges and €10.2M one-off transaction costs around the SPAC merger. Cash position €312.9M as of 27 August. Booked and awarded business €70.4M (including grants and tax credits). Delivered a 200-qubit system in Saudi Arabia and 140 qubits in Italy during the period.
- **Why it matters:** This is the first full half-year picture of a European quantum champion as a listed company, and it shows the real proportions: a few million in revenue against hundreds of millions in cash and tens of millions in losses. In the coming years the sector will be carried by capital markets and public programmes, not by customer revenue. Note the composition of the order book — grants and tax credits are included.
- **Relevance:** A sobriety anchor for regional expectations. Anyone in Brabant counting on quantum sales markets should assume government-funded system sales to computing centres and labs, not commercial volume markets. For suppliers, incidentally, that is good news: they *are* selling now.

### 8. Creotech wins €2.33M ESA contract for space-grade photon detectors

*25 Sep 2026 — [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/creotech-quantum-wins-e2-33m-2-66m-usd-esa-contract-for-space-grade-quantum-detectors/) · [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/25/creotech-quantum-qkd-system-commercialization/)*

- **What:** Creotech Quantum (Poland) leads an international consortium in ESA's HRQKD programme (High-Rate QKD) to bring superconducting nanowire single-photon detectors (SNSPDs) with integrated readout electronics to TRL 5. Duration 24 months; €1.2M goes directly to Creotech. The detectors are to serve EuroQCI, IRIS², ARTES, SAGA and ScyLight. Separately, the European Commission approved the eCAUSIS results, moving Creotech's QKD system towards commercialisation.
- **Why it matters:** Europe is steadily building its own chain for quantum communication from space, and is doing so via relatively small, targeted TRL contracts with new entrants. Poland is visibly building a position in a niche that was still open a few years ago.
- **Relevance:** Illustrates the route that is also open to Brabant detector, cryogenics and readout electronics companies: ESA TRL contracts as a stepping stone to EuroQCI supply. Manageable amounts with a clear customer.

## 2. Opportunities and calls

### EuroHPC JU — six quantum calls, €119M in total

*(Deadline: 17 November 2026, 17:00 CET — [HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/eurohpc-launches-6-quantum-calls-with-e119m-in-funding/) · [Quantum Flagship overview](https://qt.eu/funding-opportunities/))*

- Trapped-Ion Platform Technologies — €20M (HORIZON-JU-EUROHPC-2026-TIPT-09)
- Superconducting Platform Technologies — €20M, chiplet-based, 1,000+ qubits (SPT-10)
- Neutral-Atom Platform Technologies — €20M (NAPT-11)
- Next-Generation QKD Systems — €24M (NQKD-12)
- **Quantum-Testing Infrastructure — €20M (QTI-13)**: pan-European open-access test infrastructure. Connects to regional test and measurement capacity.
- **Quantum Experimental Pilot Lines — €15M (QEXP-14)**: bridging research to pilot production, TRL 4–6. Of all six, the most direct fit with the Brainport profile (fabrication, packaging, assembly).

### EuroHPC JU — three calls with a deadline this week

*(Deadline: 30 September 2026, 17:00 CEST — [EuroHPC JU](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/four-new-eurohpc-ju-calls-boost-quantum-innovation-and-standardisation-europe-2026-06-02_en))*

- [Large-Scale Photonic Quantum Computing Platform Technologies](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/large-scale-photonic-quantum-computing-platform-technologies_en) — €10M (PQC-06). The consortium must be led by a photonic quantum computing startup, with at least one large end user hosting the field demonstration. Milestones: ≥100-qubit photonic processor in 2028, full-stack system in 2030.
- Grand Challenge Quantum Sensors for Inertial Navigation — €2M (NGC-04), aimed at GNSS-denied environments.
- Standards for Quantum Technologies — €1M (STAND-05).
- *In practice:* two days remaining — realistically only relevant if a consortium is already running. Still worth noting whether Dutch parties (QuiX, TU/e, University of Twente) submit on PQC-06, with a view to joining later.

### Quantum Flagship — public consultation on the SRIA, phase II

*(Deadline: 2 October 2026 — [Quantum Flagship](https://qt.eu/news/2026/2026-09-19-phase-2-public-consultation-open-help-fine-tune-updated-SRIA.php))*

- Second consultation round on the updated Strategic Research and Industry Agenda. Besides the technology roadmaps, the new version also contains chapters on funding, IP, skills and governance. Twelve separate feedback forms, one per chapter; anonymous submission is possible.
- **Why this is worth the effort:** the SRIA steers the content of future EU calls. Targeted text suggestions on the chapters on enabling technologies, pilot lines and skills are a cheap way to get regional interests into the policy process early. Low effort, long-lasting effect — but the deadline is in four days.

### Chips JU — quantum chip design and enabling technologies

*(Deadline: 1 December 2026 — [Quantum Flagship overview](https://qt.eu/funding-opportunities/))*

- Two calls on quantum chip design and enabling technologies. Given the region's semiconductor base, the logical home call for Brainport parties; deserves its own check on the EU portal for exact scope and budgets.

**Also open:** EuroHPC Quantum Machine Learning — €6M, deadline 28 January 2027 (QML-07). Quantum Space Gravimetry — deadline 2 September 2027.

## 3. Capital, companies and talent

### Funding rounds

- **Mesa Quantum — $11.8M** *(24 Sep 2026 — [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/mesa-quantum-raises-11-8m-to-scale-chip-scale-quantum-sensors-and-alternative-pnt-infrastructure/))*. Led by Playground Global, with DCVC and J2 Ventures; total now nearly $16M in venture plus $5M in non-dilutive government funding. Chip-scale atomic clocks and quantum inertial sensors for GPS-free navigation, drones, satellites and data centre synchronisation. Based in Boulder, Colorado, expanding in New Mexico via Sandia. *Quantum sensing for PNT is currently the segment with the shortest route to defence and infrastructure budgets.*
- **Qambria — $2.4M pre-seed** *(25 Sep 2026 — [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/qambria-secures-2-4m-pre-seed-funding-to-commercialize-sub-microsecond-classical-control-infrastructure-for-fault-tolerant-quantum-systems/))*.
