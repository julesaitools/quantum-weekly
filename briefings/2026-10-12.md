*Strategic signals for Brainport Development. Every item with date and source.*

**The essence this week.** Two European policy documents landed almost simultaneously and point in the same direction: the Netherlands published its government-wide Quantum Strategy (ambition 2035, *no* new money), and QuIC published a white paper criticising Chips Act 2.0 for lacking industrial scale-up for quantum chips — with a demand profile (multi-project wafers, cryo-test, advanced packaging, visible and deep-UV photonics, small-scale fabs) that describes Brainport's capability profile almost one-to-one. Meanwhile the US put down more than $1.5 billion in public and private commitments in a single week. The difference in scale is the story; the Dutch strategy is strong on ambition and weak on industrialisation.

## 1. Top signals

### 1. Dutch cabinet publishes government-wide Quantum Strategy

*(5 October — [Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2026/10/05/kabinet-nederland-in-2035-leidend-met-quantum-en-weerbaar-tegen-risicos), [parliamentary paper](https://www.tweedekamer.nl/downloads/document?id=2026D48409), [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/10/06/netherlandss-quantum-strategy-sets-2035-goals-links-industry-growth-to-cybersecurity/))*

- **What:** Minister Herbert (Economic Affairs) and State Secretary Aerdts (Digital Economy and Sovereignty) sent the strategy to the House of Representatives, drawn up with eight other ministries. Three goals for 2035: technological leadership, less dependence on non-EU suppliers, resilience against quantum threats. Instruments: the new National Agency for Disruptive Innovation (NADI, modelled on SPRIND and ARIA), government as first customer, and a PQC migration timeline for central government (end 2026 risk policy, end 2027 migration plans, end 2030 high-risk done, end 2035 medium-risk).
- **Why it matters:** There is *no new, fully covered budget* behind it. The strategy builds on the €614.9M already awarded from the National Growth Fund to Quantum Delta NL and explicitly states that ambitions will be adjusted if funding is not found. This is an ambition document, not an investment agenda.
- **Relevance for Brainport:** This is the most important observation of the week — the strategy does not mention Brainport, Eindhoven, photonics or semiconductors. Delft is named as Europe's largest quantum cluster (about 600 jobs, €350M in private capital). The industrialisation and supply side, where Brabant has the actual capacity, is underexposed in the narrative. The implementation phase under Economic Affairs coordination is the moment to correct that.

### 2. QuIC white paper: Chips Act 2.0 lacks industrial scale-up for quantum

*(10 October — [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/quic-whitepaper-calls-for-industrial-scale-up-infrastructure-in-eu-chips-act-2-0/), [QuIC](https://www.euroquic.org/chips-act-2-quantum-position-paper/))*

- **What:** The European Quantum Industry Consortium argues that the Commission's proposal for Chips Act 2.0 (COM(2026) 504) contains no mechanisms for production-ready quantum fabrication. Five concrete asks: (1) extend Article 19 "strategic project" status to quantum foundries; (2) a subsidised Multi-Project Wafer shuttle programme with an EU-wide calendar for startups and SMEs; (3) shared cryogenic test, characterisation and advanced packaging infrastructure across 2 K, 4 K and millikelvin; (4) broaden photonics targets beyond telecom near-infrared to visible (400–800 nm) and deep-UV (295–325 nm), needed for trapped-ion and neutral-atom architectures; (5) an accessible designation for small-scale, fast-turnaround fabs that do not meet the strict "first-of-a-kind" requirement.
- **Why it matters:** QuIC argues that the six existing EU pilot lines (SUPREME, SPINS, P4Q, DIREQT, Q-PLANET, CHAMP-ION) form a basis but lack production-ready capacity — precisely MPW shuttles and open-access sub-kelvin cryo-test. And: European access to commercial quantum fabrication currently depends on conditions set outside the EU that can be revoked. As context, the paper cites IBM's commitment of more than $10bn, the formation of wafer foundry Anderon and the acquisition of HRL Laboratories.
- **Relevance for Brainport:** The demand side of this document — shared test and packaging infrastructure, small-scale fast-turnaround fabs, visible-light photonics, MPW access — describes the Brainport profile almost literally. The Netherlands already participates in three of the pilot lines (SUPREME, P4Q, SPINS; TU/e is a P4Q partner, [Quantum Delta NL, 16 March 2026](https://quantumdelta.nl/news/the-netherlands-secures-a-strong-position-on-three-european-pilot-lines)). This is the most useful policy hook of this week.

### 3. GlobalFoundries becomes the de facto foundry for quantum photonics — Xanadu partnership

*(6 October — [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/10/06/xanadu-globalfoundries-photonic-quantum-manufacturing/), [HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/xanadu-and-globalfoundries-partner-to-scale-photonic-quantum-chip-production/))*

- **What:** Xanadu (NASDAQ/TSX: XNDU) and GlobalFoundries signed a multi-year partnership for the production of photonic components on GF's 300 mm line in Malta, New York. Two components: superconducting nanowire single-photon detectors (SNSPDs) for photon-number-resolving readout, and ultra-low-loss silicon nitride (SiN) photonics. Amount not disclosed. Described as the transition from lab prototyping to industrial production.
- **Why it matters:** Three weeks earlier **SMART Photonics** (Eindhoven) and GlobalFoundries announced the first open-access foundry service for integrated silicon *and* indium phosphide photonics *(21 September — [SMART Photonics](https://www.smartphotonics.nl/knowledge-hub/smart-photonics-partners-with-globalfoundries), [Semiconductor Today](https://www.semiconductor-today.com/news_items/2026/sep/smartphotonics-210926.shtml))*. Both tracks run through the same foundry.
- **Relevance for Brainport:** This is a concrete, verifiable link between Eindhoven InP capacity and the industrialisation of photonic quantum hardware. It deserves a conversation with SMART Photonics about whether quantum is explicitly a demand segment in that GF proposition, or only datacom/AI interconnect.

### 4. US puts down more than $1.5bn in quantum commitments in one week

- **Department of War, about $350M** *(9 October — [Military Aerospace](https://www.militaryaerospace.com/computers/article/55410692/dod-announces-350-million-in-quantum-computing-initiatives))*: about $200M in new funding for DARPA's Quantum Benchmarking Initiative, plus a conditional loan of about $150M from the Office of Strategic Capital to PsiQuantum to expand its 140,000 ft² fabrication and prototyping facility in Milpitas, California — including advanced cryogenic systems ([TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/10/08/psiquantum-conditional-150m-loan-california-quantum-facility/)). DARPA selected **Atom Computing, Diraq, IBM and IonQ** for QBI Stage C, alongside Microsoft and PsiQuantum. Stage C shifts from plan assessment to government verification of hardware; the aim is to determine whether an approach reaches "utility scale" by 2033.
- **Department of Energy, $159M** *(8 October — [DOE](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-announces-new-genesis-mission-awards-advance-super-intelligence-science), [ExecutiveGov](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/doe-genesis-mission-phase-ii-projects))*: twelve Phase II Genesis Mission projects, including quantum error correction and materials co-design.
- **NVIDIA, $1bn over five years** *(8 October — [TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/10/08/nvidia-commits-1-billion-advance-us-science-next-five-years/), [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/08/3377484/0/en/nvidia-commits-1-billion-to-advance-us-science-over-the-next-five-years.html))*: for "super intelligence for science and quantum computing", with seven new systems at Argonne and Los Alamos. The quantum-specific share is not broken out.
- **Why it matters:** The US is now explicitly funding *verification and manufacturing capacity*, no longer just research. The loan structure via the Office of Strategic Capital — a capital instrument rather than a grant — is the instrumental point to watch; Europe has an equivalent in EIB/InvestEU, but hardly uses it for quantum manufacturing.

### 5. Europe assembles a sovereign stack — three moves in five days

- **Quobly and SiPearl, MoU** *(9 October — [Quobly](https://www.quobly.io/press-releases/quobly-and-sipearl-partner-to-explore-european-cpu-qpu-integration-for-sovereign-quantum-computing/), [SiPearl](https://sipearl.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/PR_SiPearl_x_Quobly_EN.pdf))*: SiPearl's Rhea1 (80 Arm Neoverse V1 cores, 61bn transistors, release end 2026, destined for JUPITER) and Seine reference server will be integrated with Quobly's silicon spin QPU on 300 mm FD-SOI, developed with STMicroelectronics. Explicit goal: bidding jointly on European tenders, including Chips JU quantum calls. Quobly raised a €115M Series A in June 2026 (Bpifrance, STMicroelectronics, SEALSQ) and is building a Si-28 chain with Air Liquide, Soitec and Orano.
- **Infineon and ZuriQ, expanded partnership** *(7 October — [TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/10/07/infineon-zuriq-scalable-quantum-chips/))*: industrialisation of ZuriQ's 2D Penning micro-trap trapped-ion chips. A 3×3 array of nine individually controlled ions was demonstrated earlier; the focus is now on significantly higher qubit counts. The design is natively two-dimensional and avoids junction structures. Amount and location not disclosed.
- **Pasqal puts Q-PLANET pilot line (€50M) into execution** *(6 October — [Pasqal](https://www.pasqal.com/newsroom/pasqal-reaffirms-its-commitment-to-the-eus-quantum-ambitions-as-it-leads-q-planet-for-neutral-atom-technologies/), [eeNews Europe](https://www.eenewseurope.com/en/pasqal-leads-e50-million-eu-neutral-atom-quantum-chip-project/))*: 28 partners from 11 countries, three years, co-funded by Chips JU plus France 2030, ERDF, Baden-Württemberg, Business Finland and FFG. Scope: lasers at 461, 698, 795 and 1013 nm, atom chips for sensing and computing, microfabricated vapour cells for atomic clocks and field sensors, and standardised Process and Assembly Design Kits.
- **Relevance for Brainport:** Q-PLANET's work packages — precision lasers at visible wavelengths, microfabrication of vapour cells, PDKs and ADKs for assembly — fall within Brabant's photonics and precision manufacturing capacity. The Pasqal release names no Dutch partners and the partner list has not been published. Worth checking: the Netherlands is in three pilot lines, but Q-PLANET (neutral atom) is not one of them.

### 6. QuantWare brings in heavyweight CFO to scale up KiloFab

*(9 October, effective 12 October — [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/whos-news-strategic-appointments-at-pqshield-vtt-quantware-and-rensselaer-polytechnic-institute/))*

- **What:** QuantWare (Delft) appoints **Bart Filius** as CFO and board member as of 12 October 2026. Filius was CFO, COO and President at Galapagos, where he led the Nasdaq listing and managed a $5bn R&D collaboration with Gilead; previously CFO of Sanofi Europe. He will lead financial strategy and capital allocation while QuantWare scales up industrial fabrication in its KiloFab facility.
- **Why it matters:** You do not hire a CFO with a Nasdaq IPO and a $5bn deal track record for incremental growth. This is the clearest signal this week that a Dutch quantum hardware manufacturer is preparing for a large capital round or listing. QuantWare is also a SUPREME pilot line partner (Josephson junction qubits and 3D integration).
- **Relevance for Brabant:** Scaling up KiloFab means demand for suppliers in packaging, cryogenic test, precision modules and assembly. That is a Brabant supply opportunity with a concrete time window.

### 7. Germany pushes ahead with its €640M competition

*(5 October — [TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/10/05/planqc-logiqc-germany-quantum-computing-competition/); background 23 September — [Quantum Spectator](https://quantumspectator.com/six-consortia-selected-to-compete-in-germanys-quantum-computing-competition-with-e640-million-in-planned-funding/))*

- **What:** Six consortia proceed in the BMFTR's Quantum Computing Competition, with up to €640M in planned funding and the aim of having at least two error-corrected quantum computers at the European technological frontier by 2030. Selected: LOGIQC (planqc, neutral ytterbium atoms, with MPQ, LMU, Tübingen, Jülich and TOPTICA), NFQC-1k (QUDORA with TU Braunschweig, Hannover, PTB, NXP Semiconductors Germany, Jülich and AQT; project volume about €122M), ATHENA-X (Peak Quantum), SPARQC (Eqcited), NEXUS (neQxt) and SuperPilot (IQM Germany with Infineon). At least one partner per consortium must have a working system meeting the entry criteria before 31 March 2027; after 30 months a new selection follows.
- **Why it matters:** One national German programme commits more money than the entire Dutch National Growth Fund quantum budget, with hard technical gate criteria and an elimination structure. No Dutch partners named. That is the relevant comparison for the Dutch 2035 ambition: the competition for European leadership is intra-European and is fought with national budgets.

### 8. Warning signal: Arqit books about $1M in annual revenue

*(9 October — [TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/10/09/arqit-preliminary-fiscal-2026-revenue-1-million/), [press release](https://investingnews.com/arqit-quantum-inc-announces-select-preliminary-financial-results-for-the-second-half-and-full-fiscal-year-2026/))*

- **What:** Preliminary, unaudited figures for FY2026 (ended 30 September): revenue about $975,000 to $1.0M, against $530,000 in FY2025. The second half, however, was about $355,000 to $380,000, *down* from $463,000 a year earlier. Cash position about $51M. Seven new contracts in FY2026. On 6 October part of a winning consortium for secure network services for NATO (one year, two extension options). Revenue loss due to ending revenue recognition at a Middle East customer.
- **Why it matters:** A listed quantum security company with a NATO contract and a $51M cash position books less than $1M in annual revenue, with a shrinking second half. A useful counterweight to the cyber and sovereignty rhetoric: the market for quantum-secure communication is mature in policy terms and barely existent commercially. Relevant to how hard Brabant parties bet on this segment.

## 2. Opportunities and calls

### EuroHPC JU — six quantum calls, €119M in total

*(Deadline: 17 November 2026, 17:00 CET — [EuroHPC JU](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/), [TQI overview](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/08/24/eurohpc-119m-funding-calls-quantum-technologies/))*

- Opened 13 August 2026, project duration 3.5 years. **Five weeks remaining.** Two calls are directly relevant for Brainport:
- **QTI-13 — Quantum Testing Infrastructure, €20M:** a pan-European open-access network of quantum test facilities, certification services (aimed at SMEs and startups) and a digital platform for remote testing. This connects directly to Brabant test and measurement capacity.
- **QEXP-14 — Quantum Experimental Pilot Lines, €15M:** experimental pilot production of quantum hardware at TRL 4–6, including processors, sensors and *cryo-compatible packaging*, linked to the Chips JU pilot lines.
- Other: TIPT-09 trapped-ion platform (€20M), SPT-10 superconducting chiplet platform (€20M), NAPT-11 neutral-atom platform (€20M), NQKD-12 next-generation QKD (€24M, more than 300 km regional coverage and hybrid QKD/PQC).

### EU Chips Act 2.0 — COM(2026) 504, under consideration in Parliament and Council

*([QuIC position paper](https://www.euroquic.org/chips-act-2-quantum-position-paper/))*

- Not a formal call, but the window for influence is open now. The QuIC asks are the most useful lever: Article 19 status for quantum foundries, an MPW shuttle programme, shared cryo and packaging infrastructure, broadening photonics targets to visible and deep-UV, and a designation for small-scale fabs. Route: via QuIC membership, PhotonDelta and Quantum Delta NL, and via Economic Affairs towards the Dutch position. QuIC also wants to align Chips Act 2.0 with the upcoming EU Quantum Act (systems, software, standards, procurement).

### NWO — Security and Resilience in a Quantum Era, €4.55M

*(Full application: 3 November 2026, 14:00 — [NWO](https://www.nwo.nl/en/calls/security-and-resilience-in-a-quantum-era))*

- Two research lines: portable entanglement for quantum networks (€1.0–1.5M, consortium allowed) and post-quantum cryptography (up to €385,000, single main applicant only). Duration up to five years. **Note:** the mandatory letter of intent closed on 29 September and NWO states that new applications are no longer possible. Only relevant for parties that have already submitted the LOI.

### Brainport Quantum Day 2026

*(2 November 2026, 10:00–17:30, Prodrive Technologies Eindhoven — [QDNL](https://qdnl.nl/events/brainport-quantum-day-2026))*

- Fifth edition, Brainport Development and TU/e together with Prodrive Technologies. Theme: from research to product, and what it takes to industrialise quantum technology. This year that theme coincides with the QuIC Chips Act 2.0 agenda and the EuroHPC QTI-13/QEXP-14 deadline of 17 November — a useful moment to organise consortium building rather than just programming.

## 3. Capital, companies and talent

### Funding and listings

- **Universal Quantum — more than $100M Series A** *(8 October — [TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/10/08/universal-quantum-raises-over-100-million-series-a/))*. Co-led by DCVC and Firgun Ventures, with EDBI, Artal (Invus), Integral GlobalTech, Main Sequence, Hostplus, NGS Super, IAG Capital, EdenBase, Eigenstate.holdings, Nauta Capital, The Venture Collective and Dave Baszucki (Roblox). Largest Series A ever for a UK quantum company. Modular trapped-ion chiplet arrays, deliberately without photonic interconnects and without large lasers and cooling infrastructure. Delivers a $77M contract to DLR. Notably, the money goes to a first R&D centre outside Europe (Singapore) plus expansion in the US and Japan, alongside the German hub in Hamburg. Capital raised in Europe, partly deployed outside Europe.
