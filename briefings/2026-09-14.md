*Strategic briefing for Brainport Development*

**The essence.** Two things changed this week. First, the US moved from subsidising quantum to owning it: Commerce finalised roughly $775M in CHIPS awards and took minority equity stakes in D-Wave, Rigetti and Quantinuum. Second, ASML entered quantum photonics manufacturing via a research collaboration with Xanadu — the single most Brainport-relevant signal of the week. Against that, Europe's answer arrived as debt rather than equity (EuroHPC + EIB), and NEC became the first major corporate to exit superconducting hardware.

## 1. Top signals

### 1.1 US Commerce takes equity stakes in quantum companies

*8–9 September — [Quartz](https://qz.com/d-wave-rigetti-quantinuum-chips-act-equity-stakes-300-million-090826) · [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/psiquantum-executes-definitive-100-million-chips-act-award-with-u-s-department-of-commerce/) · [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/08/3357666/0/en/globalfoundries-and-u-s-department-of-commerce-finalize-375m-r-d-award-to-advance-american-quantum-leadership.html)*

- D-Wave, Rigetti and Quantinuum each finalised $100M CHIPS Act R&D agreements with the US Department of Commerce, each conditioned on a **minority, non-controlling federal equity stake**. PsiQuantum executed a separate definitive $100M award (8 Sept). GlobalFoundries finalised a $375M R&D award to industrialise quantum chip production (8 Sept). Roughly $775M committed in one week.
- **Why it matters:** this is an instrument change, not a funding increase. Equity-for-subsidy makes the US government a shareholder in the quantum hardware base and signals it treats quantum as strategic infrastructure on par with leading-edge logic. It also sets a valuation floor and a de facto export-control perimeter around those firms.
- **Relevance:** Europe has no equivalent instrument. The EU Quantum Act debate now has a concrete comparator, and Dutch/Brabant scale-ups will be raising into a market where US competitors carry sovereign balance-sheet backing.

### 1.2 ASML and Xanadu collaborate on lithography for photonic quantum hardware

*9 September — [Xanadu IR](https://investors.xanadu.ai/news-releases/news-release-details/xanadu-and-asml-announce-collaboration-advance-lithography) · [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/xanadu-and-asml-partner-to-optimize-lithography-processes-for-ultra-low-loss-quantum-photonics/)*

- ASML and Xanadu announced a research collaboration to advance lithographic process nodes for photonic quantum chips. ASML contributes advanced exposure systems and computational lithography; the technical target is *Line Edge Roughness* on waveguide sidewalls, which drives Rayleigh scattering and therefore photon loss. Lower loss means lower physical-qubit overhead for fault-tolerant photonic machines. No financial terms disclosed.
- **Why it matters:** ASML has so far been an adjacent actor in quantum. This is the first clear signal that it views quantum photonics as a future lithography demand segment — and that quantum photonic yield is now a manufacturing problem, not a physics problem.
- **Relevance:** directly Brainport. It pulls the regional photonics, metrology, packaging and equipment supply chain toward a quantum-manufacturability agenda, and gives Brainport Development a credible, named anchor for convening that conversation.

### 1.3 SkyWater opens a merchant quantum foundry; IonQ launches Superion 256

*8 September — [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/skywater-launches-dedicated-quantum-solutions-merchant-foundry-division/) · [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/ionq-debuts-sixth-generation-superion-qpu-architecture-featuring-on-chip-electronic-control-and-cmos-integration/)*

- SkyWater launched **SkyWater Quantum Solutions**, a merchant foundry unit with two standard platforms: **SC250** (superconducting circuits, cryogenic interconnect and control logic) and **SP90** (silicon nitride quantum photonic routing), plus heterogeneous packaging and on-chip III-V integration (lasers, modulators, photodetectors), diamond colour centres and lithium niobate. A joint development agreement with Qolab was announced alongside.
- IonQ debuted the **Superion 256**: 256 trapped-ion qubits with Electronic Qubit Control and on-chip CMOS, replacing external laser control; rack-mountable; customer deliveries targeted 2027, volume manufacturing 2028. IonQ raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $450–460M following consolidation of SkyWater.
- **Why it matters:** quantum is acquiring the foundry/fabless split that defined semiconductors. A neutral merchant foundry with published process platforms lowers the entry cost for QPU startups — and IonQ owning that foundry is a vertical-integration move that cuts the other way.
- **Relevance:** Europe has no merchant quantum foundry of this kind. That gap is precisely what the EuroHPC pilot-line and testing-infrastructure calls (section 2) are meant to close, and it is a layer where Brainport's manufacturing base is credible.

### 1.4 NEC discontinues superconducting quantum computer development

*7 September — [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/nec-discontinues-superconducting-quantum-computer-development-to-focus-on-annealing-and-classical-emulation/)*

- NEC ended its internal superconducting quantum hardware programme, effective March 2026, citing extended commercialisation timelines and capital requirements against near-term ROI. It pivots to quantum-inspired annealing, classical emulation on HPC, and commercial optimisation services. NEC built the first controlled solid-state superconducting qubit in 1999; its exit leaves Fujitsu as Japan's principal corporate superconducting developer.
- **Why it matters:** the first major corporate withdrawal of this cycle — and it is a capital-discipline decision, not a technical failure. Expect more incumbents to conclude that owning a qubit stack is not the right position in the value chain.
- **Relevance:** reinforces a strategic point for Brabant. The durable industrial position is usually in the enabling layers (cryogenics, control, photonics, packaging, test), not in owning a qubit modality.

### 1.5 GÉANT and Quantum Internet Alliance sign MoU in Amsterdam

*8 September — [GÉANT CONNECT](https://connect.geant.org/2026/09/09/geant-and-the-quantum-internet-alliance-sign-mou-to-strengthen-europes-quantum-networking-future) · [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/geant-and-quantum-internet-alliance-partner-to-advance-european-quantum-networking-architecture/)*

- GÉANT CEO Lise Fuhr and QIA Director **Stephanie Wehner (TU Delft)** signed an MoU at GÉANT's Amsterdam offices, covering interoperability, systems and network architectures, and the move from laboratory prototypes to field trials and cross-border deployment. QIA spans academic institutions, telecom operators, system integrators and startups across 12 member states.
- **Why it matters:** it puts quantum networking onto Europe's operational research-network backbone rather than isolated testbeds. That is the step where standards, interfaces and procurement specifications actually get set.
- **Relevance:** Dutch-led and Dutch-hosted. The Netherlands retains the architectural lead in European quantum networking — a genuine positional asset when EuroQCI/QKD procurement matures.

### 1.6 Europe's counter-move: EuroHPC logical qubits and EIB venture debt

*26 August – 10 September — [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/lumi-ai-factory-selects-iqms-halocene-roadmap-for-europes-first-superconducting-logical-qubit-system/) · [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/eurohpc-joint-undertaking-selects-13-european-qpu-startups-for-quantum-grand-challenge/)*

- IQM was selected by the LUMI AI Factory (CSC, Kajaani) to deploy **LUMI-IQ**, billed as Europe's first logical-qubit-capable superconducting system: Halocene H4 with 150 physical qubits in 2027, real-time error correction in 2028, up to 9 fault-tolerant logical qubits via Halocene H5 in 2029. Co-funded by EuroHPC with Finland, Czechia, Norway and Poland (10 Sept).
- EuroHPC's **Quantum Grand Challenge** selected 13 European QPU startups (announced 26 Aug) — including two Dutch companies, **QuiX Quantum** and **Groove Quantum** — for ~€300k Phase 1 CSA projects over four months, with qualifying firms then eligible for up to €30M each in EIB venture debt from a dedicated €100M InvestEU pool.
- **Why it matters:** Europe is answering the US equity model with venture debt and procurement-led demand. Debt is non-dilutive and politically easier, but it does not put the state on the cap table or signal ownership the way the US instrument does.
- **Relevance:** two Dutch companies are inside the Phase 2 funnel. Phase 1 ends around December 2026, so their Phase 2 financing conversations start this winter — a concrete regional co-investment and soft-landing window.

### 1.7 The enabling layer consolidates: control electronics and cryogenics

*9–11 September — [Altera/Riverlane](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/altera-and-riverlane-partner-on-qec-interfaces-for-agilex-fpgas-following-post-quantum-security-rollout/) · [Quobly/Absolut](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/quobly-and-absolut-system-execute-industrial-cryogenic-roadmap-for-silicon-spin-qubit-computers/) · [Who's News](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/whos-news-strategic-appointments-at-q-ant-qtonic-quantum-quantrolox-and-leiden-cryogenics/)*

- Four items cluster: **Altera (Intel) and Riverlane** partnered on QEC interfaces for Agilex FPGAs, validating the open-source QECi protocol on Agilex 7 and publishing a reference design, supporting superconducting, trapped-ion and silicon-spin platforms (10 Sept). **Quobly and Absolut System** set out an industrial cryogenic roadmap for silicon spin-qubit machines (9 Sept). **Qubic** won a CA$1.5M Canadian federal contract for cryogenic amplifiers (11 Sept). **Leiden Cryogenics** appointed **Eric Kievit** as CEO, with founder Giorgio Frossati (company founded 1992) moving to senior scientist.
- **Why it matters:** QEC decoding is becoming a standardised FPGA interface problem, and cryogenics is being put on industrial roadmaps. Both are classic Brainport competences — deterministic low-latency electronics, thermal and mechanical systems, precision assembly.
- **Relevance:** this is the layer where regional suppliers (Prodrive, Sioux, Demcon, VDL and peers) can take share without betting on a qubit modality. The Leiden Cryogenics leadership change is also a live outreach moment for a Dutch supplier now under new commercial leadership.

### 1.8 Quantum sensing reaches expendable defence platforms

*9 September — [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/sandboxaq-and-northrop-grumman-flight-test-aqnav-quantum-navigation-on-attritable-drone/)*

- SandboxAQ and Northrop Grumman completed what is described as the first reported operational flight test of a magnetic-navigation payload on an *attritable* (expendable) drone — AQNav on Northrop Grumman's Lumberjack, with Defense Innovation Unit and NATO DIANA support. Installation and software integration took under an hour; the system provides real-time alternative PNT in GPS-denied environments, on top of 450+ prior flight-test hours.
- **Why it matters:** quantum-adjacent sensing crossed from demonstration to a cost-per-unit defence use case. Attritable platforms impose a price ceiling that forces the technology toward volume manufacturing.
- **Relevance:** read alongside the EuroHPC Grand Challenge on Quantum Sensors for Inertial Navigation (deadline 30 Sept, section 2) and the IBM/Lockheed/armasuisse offset structure below, defence procurement is emerging as a faster route to market for quantum sensing than civil applications.

### Six EuroHPC quantum platform calls

| Call | Budget | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trapped-Ion Platform Technologies (TIPT-09) | €20M | Full-stack machine, 1,000+ addressable physical qubits |
| Superconducting Platform Technologies (SPT-10) | €20M | 1,000+ qubit QPU using chiplets; explicitly includes establishing component supply chains |
| Neutral-Atom Platform Technologies (NAPT-11) | €20M | Scalable 10,000-atom simulation processors |

QEXP-14 and QTI-13 (highlighted) are the two most directly relevant to Brainport's industrial base.

## 4. Possible follow-up actions

1. **Act on the ASML–Xanadu opening.** This is the strongest hook Brainport has had for quantum photonics manufacturability. Worth testing appetite with ASML and the regional photonics/packaging/metrology suppliers for a session on quantum photonic yield and loss budgets — and using Brainport Quantum Day (2 Nov, Prodrive) as the venue. The industrialisation theme already fits.
2. **Start EuroHPC QEXP-14 and QTI-13 consortium formation now.** Deadline is 17 November; nine weeks is tight for a pilot-line or open-access test-infrastructure consortium. The Dutch assets are identifiable (TNO, QuTech, PhotonDelta, Qblox, plus Brabant manufacturing). A decision within two weeks on whether to convene or to join an existing consortium.
3. **Map the Dutch position in the 30 September inertial-navigation Grand Challenge.** Too late to originate a proposal, but knowing whether TNO/Delft/Twente are inside a consortium tells you whether the Netherlands is positioned for the EIB convertible-loan Phase 2 in quantum sensing.
4. **Open a line to QuiX Quantum and Groove Quantum before year-end.** Both qualify for Phase 2 EIB venture debt of up to €30M from around December. Useful to understand their location, facility and supply-chain needs while the financing is being structured rather than after.
5. **Contact Leiden Cryogenics' new CEO.** A leadership transition at a Dutch cryogenics supplier, in a week when cryogenics appeared three separate times as an industrialising layer, is a natural moment for a conversation about Brabant supply chain and manufacturing capacity.
6. **Worth deeper analysis: the armasuisse offset model.** Switzerland funded a national quantum system through defence procurement offset obligations. Whether a comparable instrument exists in Dutch defence procurement is a question with real potential leverage — and it needs a proper look rather than a line in a briefing.
