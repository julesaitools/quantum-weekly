*Essence: Europe had a strong week on* quantum networking *— a €47.5M second phase for the Delft-led Quantum Internet Alliance and the first entanglement-capable link over a commercial operator's deployed fibre (Delft–Rotterdam) — while the EU's* industrial *instrument slipped: the Quantum Act is now tentatively on the Commission's 18 November agenda and Chips Act 2.0 contains no quantum strategic-project priority area. In the same seven days the US stacked a federal roadmap, a $2.5B IBM fab tax deal and three state programmes. The gap this week is not research quality; it is the instrument for volume fabrication and shared test infrastructure.*

## 1. Top signals

### 1. Quantum Internet Alliance secures €47.5M for phase 2 — Delft-led

*(1 Oct — [Quantum Zeitgeist](https://quantumzeitgeist.com/qia-quantum-internet-alliance-platform-475m/), [QIA](https://quantuminternetalliance.org/))*

- **What:** The European Commission awarded QIA €47.5M for a 42-month second phase (phase 1 ran Oct 2022–Mar 2026). Target: a full-stack quantum internet prototype linking metropolitan quantum networks over long-distance fibre with quantum repeaters. Director Stephanie Wehner (TU Delft); 50+ institutions. Walton Institute joined on 2 October. Context: QIA signed an MoU with GÉANT on 8 September to use GÉANT's backbone across 40 national research networks for cross-border field trials ([QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/geant-and-quantum-internet-alliance-partner-to-advance-european-quantum-networking-architecture/)).
- **Why it matters:** Europe's quantum-internet stack stays Dutch-anchored, and phase 2 is an integration phase, not a physics phase — it buys nodes, detectors, cryogenics, control electronics, fibre integration and field-trial sites.
- **Brainport relevance:** TU/e and TNO already sit in the P4Q photonics pilot line; component supply and trial sites are the realistic entry points. Note the risk: the programme's gravity is Delft/South Holland, so Brabant value capture will not happen by default.

### 2. Eurofiber and Delft Networks distribute entanglement over deployed commercial fibre (Delft–Rotterdam)

*(2 Oct — [Quantum Zeitgeist](https://quantumzeitgeist.com/quantum-fiber-eurofiber-delft-networks/))*

- **What:** A closed-loop dark-fibre network between Delft and Rotterdam demonstrating bidirectional entanglement distribution in a real-world, non-laboratory environment, handling attenuation and synchronisation while maintaining fidelity. Delft Networks is a 2024 QuTech spin-out. Funded via the European Innovation Council, an EU Chips JU pilot, the Quantum Flagship and QDNL Delft's Regional Quantum Programme (Province of South Holland, Metropoolregio Rotterdam Den Haag, municipalities).
- **Why it matters:** A commercial telecom operator is now a quantum-infrastructure partner, not a spectator. This is the step from testbed to telco-grade.
- **Brainport relevance:** The strongest and most copyable item of the week. A Brabant metropolitan corridor (TU/e – High Tech Campus – Helmond/'s-Hertogenbosch) is now a fundable ask, and South Holland has just supplied the regional co-funding template.

### 3. NATO publishes its official quantum technology roadmap

*(1 Oct, approved 30 July — [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/nato-releases-official-quantum-technology-roadmap-establishing-operational-timelines-and-standardization-frameworks/), [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/30/nato-sets-quantum-roadmap-for-military-trials-and-secure-communications/))*

- **What:** Five lines of effort across 32 allies, with dates. Quantum sensing for positioning and navigation in GPS-denied environments, validated through the QUESTOR trials in Italy running through 2027. The Quantum Zero Beacon Project pilots a secure link between NATO HQ Brussels and SHAPE in Mons by Q4 2027. Standardisation specialist team Q3 2027, standards roadmap Q3 2029. PQC adoption ongoing; training modules within 12 months. Supported by the Transatlantic Quantum Community (22 allies plus six partners).
- **Why it matters:** This converts quantum sensing and quantum comms from research into dated procurement and interoperability requirements — the first hard timeline a supplier can plan against.
- **Brainport relevance:** Direct. The NATO DIANA accelerator sits in Eindhoven, and the ENDR dual-use conference "From Lab to Field" is in Eindhoven on 25–26 November. QUESTOR (PNT sensing) and Zero Beacon (QKD) are the two near-term doors for Brabant photonics and sensing suppliers.

### 4. US public capital compounds around physical quantum infrastructure

*(25 Sep–1 Oct — [Fermilab](https://news.fnal.gov/2026/09/doe-releases-national-quantum-computing-roadmap-following-field-wide-effort-led-by-scac-subcommittee/), [Mid Hudson News](https://midhudsonnews.com/2026/10/01/ibm-gets-pilot-agreement-from-ida-for-2-5-billion-quantum-project-2/), [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/governor-newsom-signs-fusion-legislation-and-allocates-state-capital-to-advance-california-quantum-infrastructure/), [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/roadrunner-quantum-lab-opens-35000-square-foot-albuquerque-facility-with-initial-cohort-of-quantum-startups-national-labs-and-ecosystem-partners/))*

- **What:** DOE released a national quantum computing roadmap (25 Sep) chaired by Anna Grassellino (Fermilab), targeting scientifically useful, error-corrected machines by 2028 via Quantum Grand Challenges, then a DOE Quantum Computing User Facility, then HPC/AI integration from 2030 — explicitly measuring success by scientific utility rather than qubit count. Dutchess County's IDA approved the PILOT tax agreement for IBM's $2.5B Poughkeepsie quantum facility (30 Sep–1 Oct), over objections from the Spackenkill school district. California committed $30M through a "Quantum California" initiative (1 Oct). New Mexico's Roadrunner Quantum Lab opened a 35,000 ft² facility housing 15 quantum firms with $25M in state funding, and Diraq opened an Albuquerque lab for DARPA QBI milestones (30 Sep).
- **Why it matters:** Federal roadmap + state subsidy + local tax instrument, all aimed at facilities and shared infrastructure rather than papers. Reported US Department of Commerce quantum fabrication awards in 2026 exceed $2B, including roughly $1B toward an IBM quantum foundry.
- **Brainport relevance:** This is the semiconductor playbook Brabant already runs — shared pilot and test infrastructure plus regional co-funding. The missing piece is the European instrument, which is the next item.

### 5. EU industrial instrument gap becomes concrete: Quantum Act slips to 18 November, Chips Act 2.0 omits quantum

*(18 & 29 Sep — [PostQuantum / QuIC analysis](https://postquantum.com/quantum-policy/eu-chips-act-quantum-foundry/), [MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/data-privacy-security/articles/2531216))*

- **What:** MLex reports (29 Sep) that the Quantum Act is tentatively on the Commission's 18 November agenda, with no detail disclosed and the usual caveats on impact assessment — a slip from the Q2 2026 date in the 2026 work programme, with observers flagging possible drift into 2027. Separately, the European Quantum Industry Consortium's reading of Chips Act 2.0 is that it "provides the language of industrialisation for quantum without an instrument capable of delivering it": no quantum area in Annex II (the gateway to strategic-project status), no dedicated quantum foundry mechanism, and nothing on multi-project wafer access, metrology infrastructure or licensing of pilot-line IP. No budget figures are attached pending the 2028–2034 MFF. The six existing pilot lines stay funded, including cryogenics and enabling technologies.
- **Why it matters:** Europe's pilot lines — SUPREME (superconducting), P4Q (photonics) and SPINS (semiconductor spin qubits), all with Dutch participation including TNO, TU Delft, TU Twente and TU/e — currently have no defined route to volume fabrication. Interpretation, not fact: the combination of a delayed Quantum Act and a quantum-free Chips Act annex is the single most consequential item this week for Dutch quantum hardware.
- **Brainport relevance:** There is a roughly six-week lobbying window before 18 November, and QuIC has effectively already written the argument.

### 6. Enabling supply chain consolidates around data-centre form factors

*(29 Sep–1 Oct — [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/29/xanadu-bluefors-cryogenic-infrastructure-utility-scale-quantum/), [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/infleqtion-and-riverlane-execute-strategic-mou-to-integrate-neutral-atom-hardware-with-real-time-qec-decoders/))*

- **What:** Xanadu and Bluefors formed a multi-million-dollar partnership (29 Sep) to engineer a mass-manufacturable, compact cryogenic module for single-photon detectors in data-centre environments — intended to remove the need for industrial-scale cryoplants and to serve as a blueprint for utility-scale quantum data centres. Alongside it: Infleqtion–Riverlane MOU on real-time QEC decoding with neutral-atom hardware (30 Sep); Classiq's fault-tolerance engine for hardware-aware synthesis (1 Oct); Alice & Bob with ENS Lyon replacing microwave pumps with a DC-biased SQUID for cat-qubit stabilisation (1 Oct).
- **Why it matters:** Value is migrating to thermal management, packaging, detectors, control electronics and test — and crucially toward manufacturable modules rather than bespoke lab builds.
- **Brainport relevance:** This is the closest match to Brabant's actual industrial base: precision and thermal engineering, photonic integration, test and measurement. It is a partner-development list, not a policy item.

### 7. Warning signal at home: TNO quantifies the Dutch scale-up bottleneck

*(28 Sep — [TNO](https://www.tno.nl/en/newsroom/2026/09/bottlenecks-growth-tech-start-ups), [IO+](https://ioplus.nl/en/posts/only-1-in-5-dutch-startups-becomes-a-scale-up-tno-finds-))*

- **What:** Only 21.6% of Dutch startups reach scale-up stage, against a 24.1% European average and 52.2% in the US. 24% of Dutch companies progress from Series A to Series B versus 39% in the US, and European firms take 5.8 years on average against 2.3 in the US. TNO attributes this to a stack of constraints rather than one cause: grid connection delays, technical talent shortages, insufficient testing facilities, regulatory complexity and limited late-stage growth capital.
- **Why it matters:** Quantum hardware is precisely the capital- and facility-intensive profile this report says the Netherlands handles worst. Pair it with the Crunchbase finding that quantum startup VC slowed in 2026 while public markets held strong, and private-round scarcity becomes the binding constraint for Dutch quantum hardware.
- **Brainport relevance:** It strengthens the case for shared test and pilot infrastructure plus regional growth capital over additional early-stage grant schemes — and it is Dutch, recent and quotable.

## 2. Opportunities and calls

### EuroHPC JU — six quantum calls, €119M total

*(Deadline: 17 November 2026, 17:00 CET — [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/08/24/eurohpc-119m-funding-calls-quantum-technologies/), [qt.eu](https://qt.eu/funding-opportunities/))*

- Trapped-Ion Platform Technologies (TIPT-09) — €20M, 3.5 yr, full-stack system >1,000 individually addressable physical qubits
- Superconducting Platform Technologies (SPT-10) — €20M, QPUs ≥1,000 physical qubits using chiplets
- Neutral-Atom Platform Technologies (NAPT-11) — €20M, industry-ready processors
- Next-Generation QKD Systems (NQKD-12) — €24M, extended metropolitan range
- **Quantum-Testing Infrastructure (QTI-13) — €20M**, pan-European testing and certification framework
- **Quantum Experimental Pilot Lines (QEXP-14) — €15M**, research to production-ready pilot manufacturing

QTI and QEXP are the two where Brainport partners could plausibly lead rather than join. Six weeks out, consortium positions are likely already forming — worth checking with QDNL this week whether Dutch seats are taken.

### CHIPS JU — Quantum Chips Design (RIA) and Quantum Chips: Enabling Technologies (RIA)

*(Status uncertain — verify — [qt.eu](https://qt.eu/funding-opportunities/), [Chips JU](https://www.chips-ju.europa.eu/News/))*

- [qt.eu](http://qt.eu) currently lists both calls with a **1 December 2026** deadline. A separate grant tracker lists HORIZON-JU-CHIPS-2026-QUANTUM-1-RIA (€30M) and -QUANTUM-2-RIA (€20M) as having closed on 7 and 17 September 2026. These cannot both be right — confirm on the EU Funding & Tenders portal before committing effort. Flagging this explicitly because these are the calls closest to Brainport's photonics and packaging strengths.

### Further out

- EuroHPC Quantum Machine Learning — deadline 28 January 2027 ([qt.eu](https://qt.eu/funding-opportunities/))
- Horizon Europe Quantum Space Gravimetry — opens March 2027, deadline 2 September 2027; relevant to the sensing and space supply chain
- EuroHPC hardware procurements: MeluXina-Q (call for tenders EUROHPC/2026/OP/0007, Luxembourg) and the Euro-SSQ quantum computer acquisition — supplier-side rather than grant-side opportunities ([EuroHPC JU](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/acquisition-delivery-installation-and-hardware-and-software-maintenance-euro-ssq-quantum-computer_en))

No new Dutch quantum-specific RVO scheme opened this week. Quantum Delta NL's phase 3 (€273M via the National Growth Fund) remains the main national channel.

## 3. Capital, companies and talent

### Quantum-safe security is where the deals are

*(29–30 Sep — [TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/30/quantum-emotion-acquire-plurilock-quantum-ai-cybersecurity/), [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/project-eleven-acquires-riva-labs-to-scale-post-quantum-cryptography-and-blockchain-infrastructure-stack/), [TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/29/eigenq-svaq-nasdaq-business-combination/))*

- Quantum eMotion agreed to acquire Plurilock Security. Project Eleven acquired Riva Labs to scale a PQC and blockchain infrastructure stack. EigenQ filed an S-4 with Silicon Valley Acquisition Corp to list on Nasdaq as EigenQ Holdings (EIGQ/EIGQW), closing expected Q4 2026, after roughly $45M in convertible financing of which about half is funded.
- Reading: near-term revenue and M&A activity cluster in post-quantum cryptography and quantum-safe networking, not in quantum computing hardware.
