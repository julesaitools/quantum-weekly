*Strategic scan for Brainport Development. Every item with date and source.*

**The essence this week:** two movements that intersect. Technically, the centre of gravity is shifting to *real-time classical control* of quantum systems — and a Dutch company (Qblox) sits right in the middle of the global reference stack. Financially, sentiment is cooling: analysts are talking openly about valuation discipline and consolidation. For an ecosystem made up mainly of component suppliers, that is a relevant combination: the technical position is strong, the capital window is narrowing. In addition, the **EuroHPC deadline of 17 November** for six quantum calls worth €119M in total is running — that is three weeks away.

## 1. Top signals

### 1. Qblox links control electronics to NVIDIA NVQLink — measured, not promised

*22 Oct — [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/10/22/qblox-hybrid-quantum-computing-nvidia-dgx-spark-nvqlink) · [press release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/qblox-advances-hybrid-quantum-computing-with-integration-of-nvidia-dgx-spark-leveraging-nvidia-nvqlink-302916876.html)*

- **What:** Delft-based Qblox has integrated its quantum system controller with NVIDIA DGX Spark via NVQLink. Measured round-trip latency below 4.0 µs on an RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell and below 7.2 µs on DGX Spark. That puts the GPU inside the real-time control loop: calibration during the experiment, and syndrome data to GPU decoders for real-time error correction.
- **Why it matters:** The step from architecture diagram to measured hardware performance. Error correction at scale is as much a classical control problem as a qubit problem — and that is precisely the layer where the Netherlands is strong.
- **Relevance:** A Dutch supplier is becoming part of the de facto standard stack of the world's largest classical computing player. That is the strongest industrial position the Dutch ecosystem shows this week, and it sits in the layer (control, test, interfacing) closest to Brainport's competences.

### 2. Quantinuum: 99.9995% logical fidelity on Helios — ten times better

*19 Oct — [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/10/19/quantinuum-reports-a-10x-reduction-in-logical-error-rate-on-helios/) · [Quantinuum](https://www.quantinuum.com/press-releases/quantinuum-breaks-error-correction-world-record-by-10x-validating-its-leading-path-to-fault-tolerant-quantum-computing-by-2029)*

- **What:** About five errors per million operations with a colour code, roughly 10× better than its own record of a month earlier and ~100× better than the best published result on superconducting hardware (Google). Each increase in code distance reduced the residual error by a factor of 12. Cost: up to 37 physical qubits per logical qubit (against 10 with the earlier Helix code).
- **Why it matters:** Exponential error suppression has now been demonstrated empirically, not extrapolated. Quantinuum says it is within the target range for Apollo (2029). Note the trade-off: a lower error rate in exchange for almost four times as many physical qubits — that is an architecture choice, not a free gain.
- **Relevance:** Trapped-ion is ahead of superconducting on error correction. The Dutch centre of gravity lies elsewhere (superconducting, spin, photonics). That is not a disqualification, but it does shift the story about which modality becomes usable first — relevant to how partners and investors view Dutch hardware.

### 3. EuroHPC: six quantum calls, €119M, deadline 17 November

*Open — [Quantum Flagship funding overview](https://qt.eu/funding-opportunities/) · [EuroHPC JU](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/four-new-eurohpc-ju-calls-boost-quantum-innovation-and-standardisation-europe-2026-06-02_en)*

### 4. Capital market cools: valuation discipline and expected consolidation

*24 Oct — [Evercore interview, The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/10/24/mehmet-sanliol-evercore-why-quantum-valuations-hinge-on-2030-revenue-multiples-and-consolidation/) · 22–24 Oct share price pressure: [24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/22/ionq-is-now-down-24-in-a-month-is-ionq-a-toxic-stock-or-ready-for-a-recovery/)*

- **What:** Evercore MD Mehmet Sanliol: listed quantum companies are valued on 2030/2035 revenue multiples, discounted back at ~25% (higher for private companies). Seven quantum companies are now listed; none via a classic IPO, one has filed confidentially. He expects accelerated consolidation: public shares are acquisition currency in a market with a scarce talent pool, and markets cannot keep paying for many small players with the same goal. At the same time, IonQ, Rigetti and D-Wave came under pressure this week in the broader AI sell-off.
- **Why it matters:** The narrative is shifting from "everything goes up" to "who has cash and a credible route". That is a signal about the *window*, not about the technology.
- **Relevance:** Dutch scale-ups seeking growth capital in 2027 will face a more selective market. Consolidation also means that strong Dutch component companies become acquisition targets — which touches on the investment screening question below.

### 5. Bits&Chips: "Dutch quantum enters its make-or-break phase"

*16 Oct, included in the special of 23 Oct — [Bits&Chips](https://bits-chips.com/article/dutch-quantum-enters-its-make-or-break-phase/)*

- **What:** Paul van Gerven argues that the Netherlands keeps up scientifically but risks dropping out industrially. Key points: the ecosystem consists mainly of component suppliers and lacks *system integrators* as anchor companies; the innovation toolkit is strong on research and PoC but weak on scale-up and deployment; governments should more often be *customers* (defence, secure communication, critical infrastructure, healthcare) rather than mainly grant providers. Risks named: the growth capital gap, slow permits and grid connections, and export licence procedures that are demonstrably slower than in the US, Switzerland and Israel.
- **Why it matters:** This is the sharpest public formulation so far of the problem Brainport already knows institutionally — and it places the solution in procurement policy and industrial policy, not in more research money.
- **Relevance:** The "anchor customer" argument can be used directly in regional and national conversations. The export licence complaint is concrete and testable; if Brabant companies recognise it, it is a well-founded lobbying point backed by evidence.

### 6. Toshiba launches QSN platform: hybrid QKD + PQC over existing fibre

*21 Oct — [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/10/21/toshiba-launches-quantum-safe-network-platform/) · [Toshiba](https://news.toshiba.com/press-releases/press-release-details/2026/Toshiba-launches-QSN-Platform-to-deliver-quantum-safe-networks-at-scale/default.aspx)*

- **What:** A modular platform with a shared-hub architecture that combines QKD and post-quantum cryptography over "lit fibre" — existing networks in active use.
- **Why it matters:** The cost structure of quantum-safe networks changes when you no longer need dedicated fibre. That moves QKD from pilot to tender-ready.
- **Relevance:** Touches EuroQCI-NL and pilots such as that of Falcon Systems (formerly QBird) with the Port of Rotterdam. Relevant for Brabant parties with critical infrastructure and for EuroHPC's next-generation QKD call.

### 7. Rutgers and IBM: real-time feedback control across 100 qubits

*21 Oct, Nature Physics — [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/10/21/rutgers-and-ibm-demonstrate-real-time-control-across-100-qubits/)*

- **What:** On IBM's 156-qubit Heron: nearly 5,000 two-qubit operations and as many measurements and resets, with measure-and-reset during the computation. The system switched sharply from chaotic to controllable behaviour — a predicted phase transition, confirmed far above the ~24 qubits that can be simulated classically.
- **Why it matters:** Together with the Qblox item and Quantinuum, this forms the theme of the week: error correction at scale is a control engineering problem. That is good news for anyone building measurement, control and test equipment.

### 8. New York commits $360M to regional quantum commercialisation hubs

*23–24 Oct — [Governor's Office NY](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-announces-new-study-highlighting-new-yorks-position-national-leader-quantum) · [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/new-york-deploys-360-million-for-stony-brook-and-regional-hubs-supported-by-1-37b-in-federal-foundry-commitments/)*

- **What:** A study underpins a $360M investment around Stony Brook and regional hubs, flanked by $1.37bn in federal foundry commitments. Cornell and Rochester are named as strengths.
- **Why it matters:** This is exactly the model Brainport uses — regional specialisation plus national co-funding — but at a larger order of magnitude. It is a useful benchmark and at the same time a warning about the scale at which competitors are now operating.

### Supply chain — context

Forbes published an overview of quantum supply chains *(25 Oct — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/sabbirrangwala/2026/10/25/the-global-race-for-quantum-leadership--supply-chains/))*. From the cited QED-C survey of 160 companies in 15 countries: 90% depend on at least one foreign supplier; China is the dominant supplier for about 20% of respondents, notably for optical components, InP wafers for lasers, lenses and defect-free crystals. Cryostats now cool about 1,000 qubits and take ~40 hours to reach temperature — a hard scaling bottleneck. *Caveat: China did not take part in the survey, so the dependency figures are almost certainly a lower bound.* InP wafer technology is a Brabant strength; the dependency mentioned is therefore just as much an export opportunity.
