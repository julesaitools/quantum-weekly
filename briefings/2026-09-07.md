*Strategic scan for Brainport Development. Every item is dated and sourced. Where a fact could not be verified, it is flagged as such.*

**Essence of the week.** Three of the four largest moves — Copenhagen's €390M merchant quantum foundry, the Quobly–TNO–OrangeQS agreements signed in Eindhoven, and SEEQC's cryo-chip pact with Taiwan — are bets on the same layer: **fabrication, packaging, test and yield**, not on qubit modality. The binding constraint in quantum has shifted from physics to manufacturing engineering, and the ecosystem positions being taken now are the ones that will be expensive to move later. Meanwhile the EU Quantum Act slipped again and carries no money. For Brainport the opening is narrow and specific: *test, metrology, packaging and pilot lines* — not fabrication.

## 1. Top signals

### 1. Eindhoven hosts the Franco-Dutch quantum handshake: Quobly signs with TNO and OrangeQS

*(2 September — [TNO](https://www.tno.nl/en/newsroom/2026/09/strategic-mou-quantum-computing/), [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/quobly-signs-dual-agreements-with-tno-and-orangeqs-to-industrialize-silicon-spin-qubit-manufacturing/), [Élysée](https://www.elysee.fr/en/emmanuel-macron/2026/09/02/franco-dutch-tech-dialogue))*

- **What happened.** Grenoble-based Quobly (CEO Maud Vinet; €115M Series A closed June 2026) signed two MoUs with Dutch parties, in Eindhoven, in the presence of President Macron and PM Rob Jetten. With **TNO** (Delft and Eindhoven): device measurement data, device simulation and stack-integration consulting, plus Quobly access to TNO's Quantum Information Technology Test Facility for high-throughput cryogenic characterisation and metrology on 300 mm wafers. With **OrangeQS** (Delft): adapting the OrangeQS MAX automated test platform to silicon spin qubits for high-throughput yield screening. The underlying stack is 300 mm FD-SOI CMOS from STMicroelectronics with isotopically pure Si-28 via Air Liquide/Soitec, integrated with CEA-Leti. Target: cloud/HPC deployment late 2026, one million physical qubits by 2032. No amounts disclosed. The accompanying Franco-Dutch Tech Dialogue statement commits both countries to "complementary quantum and photonics ecosystems" and to a coordinated Quantum Act.
- **Why it matters.** Silicon spin qubits are the one modality that runs on conventional CMOS fabrication, metrology and test — the layer where the Dutch and Brabant supply base is genuinely world-class rather than merely present. A French national champion has just made Dutch test capability its qualification gate.
- **Brainport relevance.** High, and immediate. TNO Eindhoven is named inside the leading European industrialisation pact for spin qubits, and a head-of-state quantum deal was staged in this region. The open question is whether the resulting integration, screening and equipment work is contracted into Brabant or stays in Delft and Grenoble.

### 2. Quantum Foundry Copenhagen: €390M for an open-access quantum chip fab

*(4 September — [Novo Nordisk Foundation](https://novonordiskfonden.dk/en/news/new-copenhagen-facility-to-strengthen-europes-quantum-manufacturing-capabilities/), [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/quantum-foundry-copenhagen-and-novo-nordisk-foundation-announce-5300-m%C2%B2-fabrication-facility-for-quantum-chips/))*

- **What happened.** The Novo Nordisk Foundation is committing DKK 2.9 billion (~€390M) to a 5,300 m² facility opening in 2027, offering commercial wafer fabrication, UHV nanofabrication, wafer-scale assembly, packaging and test — explicitly as *contract manufacturing for external quantum hardware startups across multiple qubit modalities*. Tied to the Niels Bohr Institute's NQCP programme and its 2034 fault-tolerance goal. EC Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen endorsed it in sovereignty terms.
- **Why it matters.** This is Europe's missing middle layer between lab and product, funded by a private foundation at a scale no EU public instrument has matched. A merchant, modality-agnostic foundry with packaging and test under one roof sets the default European address for quantum chip manufacturing.
- **Brainport relevance.** The most consequential competitive item of the week. Two readings: either Copenhagen becomes the node and Brainport's role narrows to equipment supply, or Brainport positions deliberately as the complementary test, metrology and advanced-packaging partner. Worth a considered position rather than a reaction.

### 3. QuTech establishes a Quantum Systems Integration Unit, targeting venture creation in 2028

*(4 September — [QuTech](https://qutech.nl/2026/09/04/qutech-establishes-quantum-systems-integration-unit/))*

- **What happened.** A dedicated Delft unit to build full-stack superconducting quantum computers from European supply-chain components on an open architecture. Leo DiCarlo (scientific lead), Vivek Sinha (strategy and partnerships), Rebecca Mayrhofer (business development), Marco Matters (QDNL CAT-1 lead). Named collaborators: TNO, Qblox, QuantWare, Delft Circuits, Orange Quantum Systems, Peak Quantum, Riverlane. Builds on HectoQubit/2, OpenSuperQPlus and five generations of Quantum Inspire systems. First-year goals: a repeatable system-integration blueprint, IP around architecture and interfaces, market studies — with venture creation planned for 2028.
- **Why it matters.** This is the moment the Dutch component cluster decides whether it becomes a machine vendor or remains a supplier to others.
- **Brainport relevance.** System integration and industrialisation is precisely what this region sells (Prodrive, Sioux, VDL, KMWE, NTS). A 2028 spin-out needs an integration partner base; that conversation should start well before 2028.

### 4. EU Quantum Act slips again — and it will not carry money

*(3–4 September — [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/04/europe-looks-to-quantum-act-to-turn-research-strength-into-industry/))*

- **What happened.** At a European Parliament event on 3 September, Thomas Skordas (Deputy Director-General, DG CNECT) said the Commission expects to present the Quantum Act by end-2026, with slippage into 2027 possible, and was explicit that "the act is not about budget… what the act will provide is the stimulus." On fragmentation: "We cannot afford having 78 startups all on their own." Tommaso Calarco (Secretary, EU Quantum Advisory Board): "This quantum act is the last train which is passing for Europe." Talent shortages were flagged specifically in software engineering, **photonics** and product management. The Parliament's legislative train still lists the proposal as Q2 2026 (missed); an inter-institutional roadmap of 23 April 2026 points to Q3 2027.
- **Why it matters.** No regulatory certainty before 2027 and no funding attached. The money continues to flow through EuroHPC JU, Chips JU and national schemes — that is where positioning effort belongs. The "critical mass" and anti-fragmentation language signals Commission preference for fewer, larger, cross-border consortia.
- **Brainport relevance.** Package regional partners into larger cross-border consortia rather than individual applications, and do not wait for the Act.

### 5. IBM Nighthawk r2: 120 qubits and a 25× jump in circuit throughput

*(2–3 September — [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/ibm-releases-ibm-nighthawk-r2-qpu-featuring-active-dissipative-qubit-reset-and-25x-circuit-throughput/), [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/03/ibms-nighthawk-r2-quantum-processor-targets-a-25-fold-increase-in-circuit-speed/))*

- **What happened.** A 120-qubit square-lattice processor with 218 couplers and 120 dedicated reset elements, executing over 100,000 circuits/second versus roughly 4,000/s for Heron, with idle time between shots cut to about one microsecond. Active dissipative reset gives a ~25× reduction in initialisation error and enables in-circuit resets, dynamic circuits and repeated error-detection rounds; IBM reports circuits above 7,500 gates. Live on IBM Quantum Platform under the IBM Phoenix system, meeting a 2026 roadmap objective.
- **Why it matters.** The competitive metric is shifting from qubit count to throughput and reset quality, because error-correction cycles are shot-limited. This raises the performance bar on control electronics latency — a component layer where Dutch and Brabant suppliers compete.

### 6. Jülich puts eleQtron's laser-free trapped-ion QPU into production on JUNIQ — on ~€81M of stacked regional and EU funds

*(4 September — [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/forschungszentrum-julich-operates-eleqtrons-jion-trapped-ion-qpu-via-juniq-infrastructure/), [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/04/julich-launches-trapped-ion-quantum-computer-for-supercomputing-integration/))*

- **What happened.** JION, an ytterbium trapped-ion processor from Siegen spin-off eleQtron, uses MAGIC (magnetic gradient induced coupling) microwave control at room temperature instead of laser addressing, and is now integrated into JUNIQ alongside the JUPITER exascale system. Disclosed funding stack: EPIQ €21M (NRW Ministry of Culture and Science), SQALING ~€25M (EU ERDF/JTF), Q-STAR.NRW ~€35M (coal-region transition fund).
- **Why it matters.** Two lessons. First, Germany keeps buying domestically built machines and wiring them into EuroHPC host sites, guaranteeing anchor customers for national vendors — the Netherlands has no equivalent QPU-at-a-national-HPC-centre arrangement. Second, and more directly useful: NRW stacked *regional transition money onto EU structural funds* to finance it. That funding architecture is replicable in Brabant.

### 7. SEEQC and Taiwan's QITPO sign an MoU to build a cryo-chip supply chain

*(3 September — [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/seeqc-signs-mou-with-taiwan-quantum-industry-technology-promotion-office-to-build-cryo-chip-supply-chain/))*

- **What happened.** Signed at SEMICON Taiwan 2026. Covers technology transfer, joint QPU/ASIC development, testing and commercialisation across Taiwan's foundry ecosystem: sub-10 K superconducting chip fabrication, cryo-CMOS ASIC interfaces, Single Flux Quantum digital qubit control, room-temperature control electronics and packaging, leveraging IC processes qualified to 4 K plus Taiwan's 3D heterogeneous integration. QITPO sits under Taiwan's Ministry of Economic Affairs. No amounts disclosed.
- **Why it matters.** Taiwan is moving to own cryogenic control electronics the way it owns logic. If cryo-CMOS qualification concentrates in Taipei, Europe inherits a second-order dependency in exactly the layer Brainport could otherwise contest.

### 8. Two quantum-safe satellite demonstrations in one week — both software overlays, neither European-won

*(1 September — [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/arqit-eshailsat-and-aiee-demonstrate-quantum-safe-satellite-encryption/); 2 September — [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/02/cyprus-greece-quantum-secure-satellite-link/), [Via Satellite](https://www.satellitetoday.com/technology/2026/09/04/sparkle-and-hellas-sat-demonstrate-quantum-satellite-connection/))*

- **What happened.** Arqit, Es'hailSat (Qatar) and AIEE (Saudi Arabia) applied Arqit's NetworkSecure and Symmetric Key Agreement platforms across the Es'hail-1 satellite with no hardware replacement in ground or space segment. Separately, Sparkle (TIM Group) and Hellas Sat ran a Quantum Safe Interconnect over a live GEO link between Athens and Cyprus — roughly 72,000 km end to end, an IPsec-based automatic key-rotation layer on existing infrastructure. Note: both are "quantum-safe" (PQC-style overlays), **not** photon-level QKD.
- **Why it matters.** The commercially realistic near-term path for quantum-safe satellite communication is a software overlay, not a QKD payload — and it will set the price expectation against which EuroQCI's QKD infrastructure is judged. The Greece–Cyprus corridor is the same one as SEEWQCI, the EuroQCI Phase II project in which the Netherlands is a formal partner (€17.8M, 50% CEF co-funded, with an optical ground station planned in the Netherlands). Also worth noting how much of this activity is landing in the Gulf rather than in Europe.

## 2. Opportunities and calls

### NWO / Ministry of Defence — Security and Resilience in a Quantum Era

*(Letter of intent: **29 September 2026, 14:00**; full proposal 3 November 2026, 14:00 — [NWO](https://www.nwo.nl/en/calls/security-and-resilience-in-a-quantum-era))*

- €4,550,000 total, verified directly on the call page. Line 1 *Portable Entanglement*: generating and storing entanglement in portable memory for mobile, energy-constrained settings — single applicant up to €1,000,000, consortium €1,000,000–1,500,000, companies and societal organisations may join. Line 2 *Post-Quantum Cryptography*: single applicant only, up to €385,000, must fund at least one PhD or postdoc. Max duration 5 years. **The LoI deadline is 22 days away — this is the most time-critical item in this brief.**

### EuroHPC JU — six quantum calls, €119M

*(Deadline: **17 November 2026, 17:00 CET** — [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/08/24/eurohpc-119m-funding-calls-quantum-technologies/), [HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/eurohpc-launches-6-quantum-calls-with-e119m-in-funding/))*

- Opened 13 August 2026; 3.5-year projects. Trapped-Ion Platform Technologies (TIPT-09, €20M); Superconducting Platform Technologies (SPT-10, €20M); Neutral-Atom Platform Technologies (NAPT-11, €20M); Next-Generation QKD Systems (NQKD-12, €24M); **Quantum Testing Infrastructure (QTI-13, €20M)**; **Quantum Experimental Pilot Lines (QEXP-14, €15M)**.
- QTI-13 and QEXP-14 are the strongest regional fits — €35M combined against exactly the test, metrology and pilot-line capability the region has. Ten weeks to deadline: enough runway to form a consortium, not much more.
- **Structural note.** Council Regulation (EU) 2026/150 of 16 January 2026 enlarged the EuroHPC JU mandate to cover quantum, and three Horizon Europe quantum calls were suspended in April 2026 as a result (HORIZON-CL4-2026-04-DIGITAL-EMERGING-11, -12, -18). Anyone still scanning HORIZON-CL4 for quantum topics is looking in the wrong place. Note also that large-scale *photonic* quantum computing lost its dedicated Horizon topic and has not reappeared among the six EuroHPC calls — a gap that matters for a photonics region.

### European Defence Fund — EDF-2026-RA-CYBER-QSTN, Quantum Secured Tactical Networks

*(Deadline: **29 September 2026** — [EC call fiche](https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/docs/2021-2027/edf/wp-call/2026/call-fiche_edf-2026-ra_en.pdf))*

- Software-defined network solutions integrating QKD (free-space and space-based), post-quantum cryptography and physical unclonable functions into military tactical networks. Results expected April 2027. *Caveat: the topic-level budget could not be verified in the retrievable part of the call fiche — check the Funding & Tenders Portal.* Three weeks out: realistically a "join an existing consortium" opportunity.

### EIC Accelerator 2026 — final cut-off of the year

*(Full proposal cut-off: **4 November 2026, 17:00 Brussels** — [EIC](https://eic.ec.europa.eu/eic-funding-opportunities/eic-accelerator_en))*

- Grant up to €2.5M plus €1–10M equity (higher via STEP Scale Up); €414M allocated to the Open track in 2026. Quantum is not among the 2026 Pathfinder Challenge topics, so the Open track is the relevant route for a quantum hardware or component scale-up. *Also: EIC Transition 2026 closes 16 September 2026 (up to €2.5M at 100%, TRL 3–4 to 5–6, must build on an eligible EU-funded project under 24 months old) — actionable only if a proposal is already largely drafted.*

### Chips JU 2026 — Quantum Chips calls (flagged, deadline unverified)

- HORIZON-JU-CHIPS-2026-QUANTUM-1-RIA (quantum chip design ecosystem and design tools) and QUANTUM-2-RIA (enabling technologies) are confirmed as call identifiers, but three national contact points give conflicting dates — 1 December 2026 as a deadline, 1 December 2026 as an *opening* with a 31 March 2027 deadline, and a 7 September 2026 deadline. **Verify on the Funding & Tenders Portal before planning around it.** What is solid: [RVO](https://www.rvo.nl/subsidies-financiering/chips-ju) confirms a Dutch Chips JU budget of €32M with **€4M earmarked for quantum**, max €5M national subsidy per project (large enterprises 20%, SMEs 30%, universities/RTOs 25%). That national top-up is a real advantage for Dutch participants and a natural brokering role for a regional development organisation.

**Also open, lower priority:** EuroHPC Grand Challenge on Quantum Sensors for Inertial Navigation (€2M, five grants, deadline 30 September 2026 — opened 2 June, not new this week, but imminent). **Closed, do not pursue:** MeluXina-Q quantum computer procurement (closed 1 September); RVO TechBridge Germany Call 2026 on quantum technologies in aerospace (closed 23 June); the three suspended HORIZON-CL4 quantum topics.

## 3. Capital, companies and talent

### Funding rounds

- **Quantum Motion — second tranche of a $160M Series C** *(3–4 September — [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/03/quantum-motion-additional-funding-silicon-quantum-computing/))*. The UK silicon-CMOS player added **Imec.ventures, Lansdowne Partners, Sony Innovation Fund, S3 Ventures and Inkef** to a round originally led by DCVC and Kembara. *The extension amount was not disclosed.* Proceeds go to custom-silicon co-development, a new Maryland lab, and DARPA Quantum Benchmarking Initiative Stage B. CEO James Palles-Dimmock argues silicon CMOS brings machine cost to "single digit millions" against >$100M for rivals. Note the Benelux angle: Imec.ventures (Leuven) and Inkef (Amsterdam) are funding a direct competitor to the Dutch and French spin-qubit efforts, on an explicitly supply-chain thesis — buy existing foundry capacity rather than build bespoke fabs.
- **Diffraqtion — $10M+ pre-seed led by Lockheed Martin Ventures** *(31 August — [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/08/31/diffraqtion-raises-10m-lockheed-martin-presidio/))*. MIT/Maryland spinout (CEO Johannes Galatsanos) building sub-diffraction-limit quantum cameras that sort light by spatial mode, claiming resolution of features up to 20× smaller than conventional systems. Presidio Ventures (Sumitomo) co-led; DARPA Direct-to-Phase-II SBIR and a NASA Phase I for orbital debris tracking in hand. The week's only substantial quantum *sensing* funding event — and a reminder that Europe has the photonics capability but no comparable prime-contractor venture channel.

### Partnerships and corporate action

- **Quantinuum – Aramco, non-binding MoU on energy-sector quantum R&D** *(3 September — [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/quantinuum-and-aramco-execute-non-binding-mou-for-energy-sector-quantum-rd/))*. Explicitly non-binding; read as pipeline signalling to public markets following Quantinuum's $1.68bn Nasdaq IPO in June 2026, not contracted revenue.
- **Pasqal – KACST multi-year PQC research programme, Saudi Arabia** *(31 August — [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/pasqal-and-kacst-partner-to-advance-post-quantum-cryptography-in-saudi-arabia/))*. No budget disclosed. A flagship European champion routing commercial expansion and now cryptography research through Gulf sovereign funding, weeks after listing on Nasdaq rather than a European exchange — the precise capital-and-market-access problem the Quantum Act is meant to address.
- **George Mason University and TreQ — $7.7M open-architecture QPU for Virginia** *(3 September — [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/george-mason-university-partners-with-treq-to-install-7-7m-open-architecture-quantum-qpu-in-virginia/))*. Oxford-based TreQ supplies its Open Architecture Quantum framework with interchangeable QPU, cryogenics, control electronics and compilers; commissioning late summer 2027. Deliberately anti-lock-in procurement, and a route to market for independent component suppliers — a procurement template European regions could copy.
- **PsiQuantum – Brookhaven National Lab** *(2 September — [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/psiquantum-and-brookhaven-national-lab-partner-to-develop-fault-tolerant-algorithms-on-construct-platform/))*. Brookhaven adopts PsiQuantum's Construct resource-estimation and compilation platform under DOE's Quantum Genesis initiative. Whoever's compiler the national labs standardise on shapes which architecture algorithms get written for.
- **Xanadu roadmap to 1,000+ logical qubits by 2031** *(31 August — [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/08/31/xanadu-1000-logical-qubits-2031/))*. First credible photonic roadmap pegged to logical-qubit counts on IBM/Google timescales, with ~US$686M available funding including US$140.2M of Canadian government money via Project OPTIMISM. State-underwritten photonics — a template European policymakers are studying.

### Warning signals

- **No layoffs, shutdowns, down-rounds, cancelled projects or short-seller reports were verifiable in this window.** The two credible cautions are structural.
- **ERVA's Brian Gaucher: "The window closes in the single digit years."** *(5 September — [The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/05/brian-gaucher-erva-why-engineering-not-physics-now-limits-quantum-progress/))*. The IBM veteran and ERVA report co-chair argues engineering, not physics, is now the binding constraint: "Once manufacturing ecosystems become geographically concentrated, they're going to be difficult to move and costly to rebuild." He is careful not to overstate it — "I don't think the house is on fire… strategically urgent, but not technically desperate" — while noting cost remains unresolved and scalability unknown. This is the sharpest available framing of the Brainport thesis: the bottleneck is lab-to-fab, meaning reliability, yield and manufacturability.
- **The timing contrast is the signal.** In the same seven days that the EU's flagship instrument slipped with no money attached, a Danish private foundation committed €390M to a fab and Taiwan moved to lock in cryo-CMOS. The capital gap is being filled by non-EU and private actors while the framework is still being drafted.

## 4. Possible follow-up actions

1. **Decide on the NWO call this week.** The letter of intent for *Security and Resilience in a Quantum Era* is due 29 September — 22 days. Line 1 (portable entanglement, consortia allowed, up to €1.5M) suits a combination of regional photonics hardware with a defence or security end user. If any Brainport partner wants in, the LoI has to be moving now.
2. **Start consortium formation for EuroHPC QTI-13 and QEXP-14 (17 November).** €35M combined across quantum testing infrastructure and experimental pilot lines — the two topics of the six that map onto what the region actually has. Ten weeks is workable but not generous.
3. **Convert the Quobly momentum into Brabant supply-chain work.** TNO Eindhoven and OrangeQS now have a French national champion depending on their test and screening capacity. Approach both about which integration, equipment and high-throughput screening work could be contracted regionally — and use [Brainport Quantum Day, 2 November at Prodrive Technologies](https://qdnl.nl/events/brainport-quantum-day-2026), whose theme is industrialising quantum technology, as the venue to put regional suppliers in front of that demand.
4. **Take a deliberate position on Copenhagen.** Quantum Foundry Copenhagen opens in 2027 as a merchant fab with packaging and test. Decide within the next quarter whether Brainport's line is complementary (test, metrology, advanced packaging, equipment) or competitive, and open a conversation with the foundry on that basis rather than letting the default answer settle by itself.
5. **Study the NRW funding architecture.** Jülich's JION was financed by stacking regional transition money (~€35M coal-region fund) onto EU structural funds (~€25M ERDF/JTF) and state research money (€21M). That is a replicable model for financing quantum infrastructure in Brabant, and worth a short analysis before the next round of regional programming.
6. **Verify the Chips JU quantum deadlines directly** on the Funding & Tenders Portal, given the €4M Dutch quantum earmark and up to €5M national subsidy per project. Sources currently conflict; the answer determines whether this is a 2026 or 2027 action.
