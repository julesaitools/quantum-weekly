*Strategic briefing for Brainport Development*

**The essence:** This week the US turned quantum chip manufacturing into state-backed industrial infrastructure. It finalised about $1.5B in CHIPS awards to quantum foundries (Anderon/IBM, GlobalFoundries, Rigetti) and opened a $215M race for 100 logical qubits. In Europe the signal is industrialisation of silicon spin qubits on 300mm lines (Quobly/ST, Quantum Motion). Meanwhile NVIDIA's CUDA-Q Logical is becoming the default integration layer for the stack. For Brainport, the question is sharper now: does Europe get its own quantum fab and supply-chain capacity, or does hardware gravitate to US foundries? Three EuroHPC calls close on 30 September.

## 1. Top signals

### 1. US finalises CHIPS awards for quantum foundries: Anderon/IBM $1B, GlobalFoundries $375M, Rigetti $100M

*(14–18 Sep — [QCR: Anderon](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/anderon-finalizes-1-billion-chips-act-award-to-scale-300mm-pure-play-quantum-wafer-foundry/), [QCR: GlobalFoundries](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/globalfoundries-finalizes-375-million-chips-act-award-for-onshore-quantum-semiconductor-fabrication/), [QCR: Rigetti](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/rigetti-computing-finalizes-definitive-100-million-chips-act-award-with-u-s-department-of-commerce/))*

- **What happened:** IBM subsidiary Anderon got $1B from Commerce plus $1B from IBM for an open, pure-play 300mm quantum wafer foundry in Albany, NY. First production lots are already running. GlobalFoundries got $375M over five years for its Quantum Technology Solutions unit in Malta, NY, using the 22FDX node for cryo-CMOS control. Rigetti got $100M. All three are part of a $2B CHIPS quantum allocation across nine developers. Separately, IBM is pursuing a $2.5B quantum facility in Poughkeepsie ([Mid Hudson News, 14 Sep](https://midhudsonnews.com/2026/09/14/ibm-seeks-pilot-for-2-5-billion-quantum-computing-facility/)). Photonic Inc. proposed a CA$500M quantum/AI/defence fab in Canada ([QCR, 14 Sep](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/photonic-inc-unveils-project-vanguard-proposal-for-ca500m-us359-1m-semiconductor-facility-in-canada/)).
- **Why it matters:** These are signed commitments, not announcements of intent. The US is building open foundry capacity that any hardware startup, including European ones, can use. That creates a pull effect on European fabless quantum companies.
- **Relevance:** This bears directly on Brainport's semiconductor supply chain: lithography, metrology, packaging, cryo-CMOS and test. It is both an export opportunity for regional suppliers and a strategic risk if European fab capacity lags. It strengthens the case for positioning Brainport in the Chips Act 2.0 and Quantum Act debate (see signal 6).

### 2. DOE launches $215M "Quantum Genesis Q" race for 100+ logical qubits

*(17 Sep — [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/doe-launches-215m-quantum-genesis-q-competition-and-45m-vv-lab-call/), [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91608672/the-energy-department-launches-a-215-million-push-to-make-quantum-computers-scientifically-useful-by-2028))*

- **What happened:** The competition is modality-agnostic. It pays up to $215M: Phase I up to $1.5M per awardee, then a $100M Phase II pool plus two $50M bonus pools for 150 and 200 logical qubits. A separate $45M call funds a quantum-HPC validation testbed. There is an applicant webinar on 25 Sep and submissions are due 19 Oct. The target is scientific usefulness by 2028. A DOE panel also tied a future national quantum facility to proof of scientific use ([TQI, 19 Sep](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/19/doe-panel-ties-future-national-quantum-facility-to-proof-of-scientific-use/)).
- **Why it matters:** Public procurement is moving from "physical qubits" to logical qubits and verified utility. That raises the bar for every hardware player, including European ones competing for the same customers.
- **Uncertainty:** The sources do not say whether non-US companies can take part.

### 3. Silicon spin qubits move onto industrial 300mm lines in Europe: Quobly/STMicro and Quantum Motion

*(14–16 Sep — [QCR: Quobly](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/quobly-demonstrates-single-chip-readout-and-gates-on-300mm-industrial-silicon-process/), [Quobly press release](https://www.quobly.io/press-releases/quobly-demonstrates-key-quantum-operations-on-single-industrial-silicon-chip-advancing-toward-large-scale-quantum-computing), [QCR: Quantum Motion](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/quantum-motion-finalizes-second-close-of-series-c-funding-round-for-silicon-qpu-scaling/))*

- **What happened:** Quobly (France) showed readout, single-qubit gates and two-qubit gates on one chip. The chip was made on STMicroelectronics' 300mm production line in Crolles, with Si-28 supply from Air Liquide, Soitec and Orano. The Alloy Pioneer cloud system is planned for late 2026. Quantum Motion (UK) closed the second tranche of its Series C (initially $160M). DCVC and Kembara co-lead, with **Inkef** (NL) and **imec.ventures** (BE) among the participants. Quantum Motion also integrated with CUDA-Q Logical and demonstrated a cryogenic QEC decoder on commercial FPGAs.
- **Why it matters:** Of all qubit modalities, spin qubits fit existing semiconductor manufacturing best. That is where Europe, and the Netherlands in particular, has an edge.
- **Relevance:** TNO signed an MoU with Quobly on 2 Sep ([TNO](https://www.tno.nl/en/newsroom/2026/09/strategic-mou-quantum-computing/)). The SPINS pilot line is at imec. Brainport semiconductor players fit naturally into this value chain.

### 4. QuTech spinout Q\*Bird becomes Falqon Systems and moves into end-to-end quantum-secure networks

*(16 Sep — [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/qutech-spinout-qbird-rebrands-to-falqon-systems-to-commercialize-quantum-secure-networks/))*

- **What happened:** The Delft MDI-QKD company (CEO Ingrid Romijn) is moving from hardware components to a full stack: MDI-QKD hardware, a hybrid QKD/PQC key manager and a network domain controller. It has deployments in NL, BE, LU, IE, AT and ES, including MadQCI in Madrid. Ownership is unchanged. In the same week, Q-SAFE (Germany) launched a carrier-grade QKD+PQC platform that runs over existing fibre and meets EuroQCI procurement requirements ([QCR, 17 Sep](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/q-safe-solutions-unveils-eu-27-carrier-grade-quantum-safe-network-platform/)).
- **Why it matters:** The EuroQCI market is moving from demos to procurement. Vendors are positioning on "sovereign, made in Europe" and hybrid QKD+PQC.
- **Relevance:** Falqon is one of the few Dutch quantum companies with international network deployments. It is a candidate for regional pilots with telecom, energy or defence, and for Brainport Quantum Day.

### 5. NVIDIA CUDA-Q Logical becomes the integration layer for fault-tolerant quantum computing, with many European firms signing on

*(14 Sep, IEEE Quantum Week — [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/nvidia-unveils-cuda-q-logical-to-accelerate-fault-tolerant-system-orchestration-across-hardware-modalities/))*

- **What happened:** NVIDIA released a framework that ties together algorithms, error correction and hardware. On day one, integrations came from IQM, Quantum Motion, QC Design, Quandela (via NVQLink), Qedma, Quantum Machines, Infleqtion, Iceberg/Diraq, Anyon and others.
- **Why it matters:** NVIDIA is taking control of the quantum-HPC interface layer, as it did with CUDA in AI. That is convenient for startups, but a dependency for sovereignty.
- **Relevance:** The Dutch open-architecture approach (Quantum Inspire, Qblox, QuantWare, Orange QS) needs a clear position on interoperating with this layer. It is a good topic to discuss with QDNL and QuTech.

### 6. European policy: QuIC pushes quantum into the Chips Act 2.0; Quantum Act may slip to 2027

*(17 Sep — [QuIC whitepaper](https://www.euroquic.org/quantum-in-the-chips-act-2-0-quic-whitepaper/); context 4 Sep — [TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/04/europe-looks-to-quantum-act-to-turn-research-strength-into-industry/))*

- **What happened:** QuIC's response to COM(2026) 504 (Chips Act 2.0) asks for production-grade quantum fab capacity, multi-project wafer access, and support for enabling technologies: photonics, cryogenic testing, packaging, metrology and design enablement. It also asks for alignment with the Quantum Act. According to DG CNECT, the Commission plans to present the Quantum Act by the end of 2026, possibly slipping to 2027. DG CNECT describes it as an "enabling framework" without its own budget.
- **Why it matters:** Coming the same week as the US CHIPS awards, it highlights the gap: the US is paying out while the EU is still framing policy.
- **Relevance:** QuIC's list of priorities overlaps almost exactly with Brainport's strengths. There is a window to feed in regional input through QDNL, PhotonDelta and Holland High Tech while the Chips Act 2.0 and Quantum Act are still being shaped.

### 7. Warning signals: architecture uncertainty and questions about scaling

*(14–17 Sep — [QuEra survey](https://www.quera.com/press-releases/quantum-market-wants-a-clear-path-to-fault-tolerance-as-architecture-race-remains-unsettled-quera-survey-finds), [TQI study](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/15/quantum-computers-need-100000-fold-performance-gain-for-scientific-utility-study-finds/), [PostQuantum on Fujitsu](https://postquantum.com/industry-news/fujitsu-diamond-spin-snv-prototype/))*

- **What happened:** A QuEra survey found the market sees no clear path to fault tolerance yet. A study estimates that a roughly 100,000-fold performance gain is needed for scientific utility. PostQuantum criticises Fujitsu's diamond-spin prototype, which was developed with QuTech/TU Delft ([QuTech, 9 Sep](https://qutech.nl/2026/09/09/diamond-spin-quantum-computing-takes-a-step-toward-optical-scaling/)), for missing scaling data. NEC says practical quantum computers are still more than 10 years away ([Sutor digest, 19 Sep](https://drbobsutor.substack.com/p/daily-quantum-update-for-saturday-085)).
- **Interpretation:** Capital and public money are flowing faster than the technical proof points. This argues for a portfolio approach, with a focus on modality-agnostic enabling technologies, which is where Brainport sits.
